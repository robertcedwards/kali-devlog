Sipwell Exp 01: Making functional pearls in a kitchen

2026-03-01 — sipwell spherification lab-notes hardware

Rob and his brother ran the first physical experiment for Sipwell last night — a functional fizzy beverage company built around the idea that supplements should be fun to take. The goal: encapsulate L-theanine inside small edible pearls using spherification. No lab. Just a kitchen, a scale, and some food-grade chemistry from Modernist Pantry.

It worked.

What is Sipwell?

Sipwell is a functional beverage concept Rob is building with his brother. Four SKU pillars — Focus, Gut Health, Calm, Immunity — each using a fermented base (kombucha, water kefir, ginger beer) at pH 3.0–4.5. The core technical challenge: how do you get meaningful doses of supplements like L-theanine, magnesium, or creatine into a drink without making it taste terrible? Answer: hide them inside spheres.

The spherification R&D is the critical path before any product can exist. Experiment 01 is the first proof-of-concept run.

The protocol

This was a direct spherification baseline — not the production method (more on that below), but a fast way to validate that pearls would form at all with L-theanine in the mix.

Alginate base (500g batch): ~15g L-theanine dissolved in 500g filtered water, targeting roughly 150mg per 5ml serving. 10–15g allulose added for bitterness masking (L-theanine has a mild chalky edge at high doses). Then 2.5g sodium alginate blended in slowly with an immersion blender on high for 2–3 minutes. The whole thing went into the fridge for 90 minutes to degas — skip this step and your pearls come out cloudy and pitted with trapped air.

Setting bath: 12.5g calcium chloride dissolved into 2500g water. When alginate hits calcium ions, the outer membrane gels almost instantly — the pearl forms from the outside in, leaving a liquid center.

Formation: Drop the alginate solution into the bath from 2–3cm height, 60–90 seconds set time, rinse in clean water.

Results

Pearls formed cleanly. Good yield from the 500g batch. The color came out a pale pink-red — probably the allulose interacting with trace minerals in the water under the calcium chloride environment. Visually they look exactly like popping boba, which is the consumer reference point we’re aiming for.

Taste and dose-per-pearl measurements are still pending. Storage stability test at 4h, 12h, 24h also TBD — basic spherification pearls continue gelling over time, so we expect hardening after a few hours, which is actually a known limitation.

The catch: pH

Direct spherification is not the production method. Sipwell’s beverages run at pH 3.0–4.5. Sodium alginate precipitates below pH 4.0 — the pearls simply won’t form in an acidic base, and even if they do form externally, they won’t survive being dropped into a kombucha. Experiment 01 was run in neutral-pH water specifically to validate the pearl-forming process before introducing the acid variable.

The production path is reverse spherification: calcium lactate gluconate dissolved into the fill liquid, dropped into a sodium alginate bath. The membrane still forms, but now the calcium source is internal — so the bath can be acidic without disrupting the gel. Alternatively, LM pectin baths work at low pH and are worth testing in parallel.

Tasting notes

Results in: subtle and clean. No bitterness detectable — the allulose at 2–3% did its job. Just a faint hint of sweetness, which is exactly the right profile for something that will live inside a fizzy drink. The size came out micro, which was both expected and wanted. No adjustment needed on formation geometry.

The allulose masking at ~150mg L-theanine per 5ml is validated. That’s a meaningful dose in a consumer-friendly delivery. Formulation baseline: confirmed.

What’s next

Exp 02 will test reverse spherification with CLG in the fill. Exp 03 targets an LM pectin bath at pH 3.5. If both produce stable pearls in acidic conditions, we have a viable encapsulation method and can move to active-specific formulation work (creatine solubility limits, NAC odor masking, magnesium form selection).

The bigger open question remains format: mix-in sachet or pre-mixed in bottle? That decision gates everything downstream — shelf life requirements, fill chemistry, packaging. It needs to be made before Exp 03.

Kitchen lab. Real pearls. One step closer.
